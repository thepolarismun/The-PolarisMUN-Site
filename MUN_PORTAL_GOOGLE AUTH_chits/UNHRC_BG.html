UNHRC Background Guide 2
UNHRC — Human Rights Council Rules of Procedure
Section I
Rules of Procedure:
Points & Yields
  • Point of Information (POI): Used to ask a question to a delegate who has just finished their speech.
  • Point of Order (POO): Used to correct a procedural error or factual inaccuracy made by the Chair or another delegate.
  • Point of Personal Privilege (POP): The only point that can interrupt a speaker; used to address immediate personal discomfort.
  • Yielding Time: Delegates can yield remaining time to POIs, the Chair, or another delegate.
Executive Board Note

Yielding to another delegate will not fetch you marks for your own time.

UNHRC Background Guide 3
UNHRC — Human Rights Council Committee Flow
Section II
Committee Flow

Roll Call & GSL

Delegates must state “Present” or “Present and Voting”. Debate begins with the General Speakers' List where first-person pronouns are prohibited.

Chits

Delegates must actively utilize written chits to coordinate, lobby, and submit arguments during committee.

Formal Chits

Used to communicate through the Executive Board without interrupting oral debate.

Substantive Chits

Sent to the Executive Board to convey speech points, arguments, and proposed solutions.

Motion Format

“The delegate of USA would like to move the committee to a moderated caucus with a total time of 30 minutes and a per-speaker time of 90 seconds for the topic [XYZ].”

UNHRC Background Guide 4
UNHRC — Human Rights Council Core Agenda
Section III
The Core Agenda:
Border Enforcement

Global migration has reached unprecedented levels due to conflict, poverty, and climate change, clashing directly with stricter state border controls.

Immigration Enforcement

The criminalization of migration and systematic targeting of undocumented individuals.

Border Control & Pushbacks

Physical barriers and high-tech surveillance forcing migrants into dangerous routes.

Migrant Detention

Overcrowded and unsanitary detention centers holding asylum seekers and children.

Human Dignity

The debate ultimately centers on balancing national sovereignty with fundamental human rights.

UNHRC Background Guide 5
UNHRC — Human Rights Council Human Rights Framework
Section IV
Human Rights Framework & Affected Groups

Migrants

Individuals moving for economic or family reasons whose dignity and rights must remain protected.

Refugees

Individuals fleeing persecution and entitled to specific protections under international law.

Asylum Seekers

Individuals awaiting decisions on asylum claims and highly vulnerable to detention.

Legal Pillars

Includes non-refoulement and the right to seek asylum under Article 14 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

UNHRC Background Guide 6
UNHRC — Human Rights Council Real-World Cases
Section V
Real-World Cases & State Violations

Third-Country Deportations

Migrants intercepted and transferred to third-party nations in exchange for financial or diplomatic agreements.

Asylum Shutdowns

Suspension of asylum access at borders leading to increased migrant deaths along dangerous transit routes.

Zone Pushbacks

Temporary suspension of asylum rights in designated exclusion zones near borders.

International Debate

Delegates are encouraged to analyze these precedents critically while framing policy recommendations.
