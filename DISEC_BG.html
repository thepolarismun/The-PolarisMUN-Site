DISEC Background Guide

Gray Zone Warfare, AI Disinformation & Arctic Militarization

Examining the intersection of gray zone warfare, AI-enabled disinformation campaigns, cyber operations, and Arctic militarization within evolving international legal and strategic frameworks. For Delegate Use Only.

Section 01

Director's Note & Marking Policy

This committee operates under a partial crisis format. Questions posed during debate must be grounded in a specific incident, treaty article, legal case, or verifiable statistic.

  • Open-ended policy questions without evidence-based framing will receive negative marking.
  • Delegates are expected to cite incidents, treaties, and legal loopholes precisely.
  • Exposing contradictions or factual inaccuracies during debate is encouraged and rewarded.
  • The guide focuses on operational gaps in international law rather than introductory theory.
Section 02

Key Legal Instruments & Loopholes

UN Charter Art. 2(4)

Traditional definitions of armed force exclude cyberattacks, economic coercion, and information warfare.

UN Charter Art. 51

Cyber operations such as NotPetya caused global-scale damages yet failed to trigger the armed attack threshold.

UNCLOS Arts. 37–38

Russia and Canada assert internal waters claims over Arctic routes while Western states classify them as international straits.

Svalbard Treaty Art. 9

Modern radar and satellite systems exist in a legal grey area not anticipated by the treaty.

Section 04

Arctic Strategic Corridors & Disputes

Northern Sea Route

Russia claims the NSR as internal waters requiring prior notification and escort.

Northwest Passage

Canada asserts historic internal waters claims while others classify the route as an international strait.

GIUK Gap

The Greenland–Iceland–UK corridor remains strategically critical for NATO maritime defence.

Lomonosov Ridge

Russia, Canada, and Denmark maintain overlapping continental shelf claims.

Section 06

Portfolio Positions — All 30 States

Verified positions as of 2025–26. Bold text indicates specific incidents, treaty references, and instruments to cite. These are your research floor — not your ceiling.

Russia

Arctic P5 · CSTO · Northern Fleet: Severomorsk. World's largest icebreaker fleet, including the only fleet of nuclear-powered icebreakers. Claims NSR as internal waters; deployed S-400 along NSR and new Arctic bases since 2014. GRU cyber operations: APT28, Sandworm, SVR SolarWinds. Frames all this under its 2022 Maritime Doctrine as defensive responses to NATO encirclement. Expanding Arctic militarization while rejecting binding attribution frameworks.

United States

Arctic (Alaska) · P5 · NATO · Five Eyes · NORAD. 2024 DoD Arctic Strategy listed China — not Russia — as primary Arctic concern. NORAD intercepted Russian aircraft near Alaska 9 times in 2025. Attributed NotPetya and SolarWinds. Focused increasingly on China as the primary Arctic concern while supporting stronger cyber attribution mechanisms.

Canada

Asserts Northwest Passage as internal waters. Managed via 1988 Arctic Cooperation Agreement. NORAD modernization includes CAD$38.6bn plan. Will resist any framework conceding the Northwest Passage as international strait.

Norway

Only NATO ally with direct land frontier with Russia in the Arctic. Hosts US Marine Corps rotation and F-35 basing. Documented GPS jamming of Norwegian civil aviation between 2022–2025.

Denmark (Greenland)

Pituffik Space Base anchors the GIUK gap. Greenland’s Self-Governance Act gives Nuuk control over most domestic affairs. Must balance NATO obligations, Greenlandic independence trajectory, and US strategic pressure simultaneously.

Iceland

NATO member with no standing army. Dependent on US/NATO for defence. Advocates maintaining Arctic Council engagement even amid Russia’s suspension.

Finland

NATO accession added 1,340km of NATO-Russia border. Hosts NATO-accredited Hybrid CoE in Helsinki. Finland’s comprehensive security model integrates civilian and military hybrid response.

Sweden

NATO accession followed Russian and Turkish pressure campaign. FRA among Europe’s most active agencies in technical attribution of cyber operations. Gotland Island now key NATO Baltic flank position.

United Kingdom

UK MoD stated Russian naval vessels actively mapping NATO seabed in High North. Salisbury Novichok attack and multiple GRU-linked incidents shape UK policy. Strong advocate for attribution accountability.

France

Subject of first formal ITU RRB condemnation for Russian satellite interference. Primary driver of EU AI Act and DSA. Active voice on AI governance and disinformation accountability.

Germany

Nord Stream 1 & 2 destruction remains central security concern. Hosts NATO Cyber Rapid Reaction Teams. Leads EU hybrid threat policy coordination.

Italy

Operates Dirigibile Italia research station at Ny-Ålesund. Target of Russian and Chinese disinformation campaigns. Supports governance frameworks with proportionality safeguards.

Netherlands

Expelled 4 GRU officers after OPCW hack attempt. MH17 Joint Investigation Team led to Russian convictions in absentia. Strongest voice for attribution accountability.

Poland

Belarus-engineered border migration crisis cited as gray zone coercion. Highest NATO defence spending percentage in 2024. Views gray zone operations as aggression under Article 51.

Turkey

Blocked Swedish/Finnish NATO accession for 20 months. Purchased Russian S-400 systems. Controls Black Sea access through Montreux Convention.

China

Self-declared near-Arctic state. Promotes Polar Silk Road concept. Opposes attribution mechanisms and emphasizes internet sovereignty. Promotes Polar Silk Road initiatives and opposes external attribution systems under sovereignty arguments.

India

Arctic Observer since 2013. Operates Himadri station at Ny-Ålesund. Will act as bridge-builder between Western and non-Western positions.

Japan

2022 National Security Strategy identified hybrid threats as major challenge. Supports strong cyber norms and transparency measures.

South Korea

Target of North Korean GPS jamming and cyberattacks. Operates icebreaker Araon for annual Arctic missions.

Australia

Multiple cyberattacks publicly attributed to China. Among strongest advocates for binding attribution frameworks.

Switzerland

Hosts ITU headquarters, ICRC, and UN Human Rights Council. Potential host for Arctic military dialogue mechanisms.

Austria

Hosts OSCE headquarters and Vienna Document confidence-building framework. Well positioned to propose Arctic CBM mechanisms.

Ireland

Western coast contains major transatlantic fibre optic landing points. Will advocate strongly for undersea infrastructure protection.

Belgium

Hosts NATO headquarters and EU institutions. Advocates strengthening existing institutional mechanisms.

Ukraine

Primary gray zone warfare victim. Will push for strongest possible attribution and accountability language. Frames gray zone warfare as a direct precursor to kinetic conflict.

Iran

IRGC-linked units sanctioned for FIMI operations. Will align with Russia and China on sovereignty objections.

North Korea

Lazarus Group cyber thefts fund WMD programmes. Will obstruct frameworks constraining cyber capabilities.

Belarus

Forced down Ryanair FR4978 and engineered migration crisis at EU borders. Mirrors Russia’s positions closely.

Saudi Arabia

Target of Iranian gray zone attacks including Abqaiq-Khurais strike. Potential swing vote on disinformation accountability frameworks.

UAE

Ran Project Raven offensive cyber operations. Seeking role as neutral AI governance hub while protecting offensive capabilities.
